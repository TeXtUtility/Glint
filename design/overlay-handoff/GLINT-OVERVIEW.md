# Glint: What It Is and What It Does

On 25 September 2026, Glint was committed as a private macOS app at version 0.2.0, and within two days it had reached version 0.8.0, adding speaker recognition, a minimal "Glance" layout, automatic AI fallbacks and in-app updates along the way. Glint is an AI assistant for meetings that sits on top of whatever you are doing: a small translucent bar at the top of the screen, with a chat panel beneath it, which listens to both sides of a call, keeps a live transcript, and answers questions about the conversation and about what is on your screen when you press a hotkey. It belongs to the same category as the real-time "meeting copilot" tools that have appeared in the last few years, but with one deliberate difference: there is no Glint server, no account and no subscription, so everything runs on your own Mac and talks directly to an AI provider you choose.

## Who it is for

Glint is built for anyone who spends a large part of their day in calls and would benefit from a second pair of eyes that has heard every word. The eleven built-in modes show the situations it was designed around: job interviews (general, behavioural, coding, system design, case, and recruiter screens), interviewing a candidate, sales calls, team meetings, lectures, and coding help. A candidate can ask it how to structure an answer, a salesperson can ask for an honest reply to an objection, a manager can ask where a discussion stands and who owns what, and a student can ask it to explain the slide the lecturer just moved past. Outside of calls, it works as a general assistant that can see your screen, so it is equally useful for a quick question about an error message or a document.

## What using it looks like

A typical call starts with a shortcut (⌘⇧\\) that begins a session, after which Glint transcribes you and the other participants as the conversation happens, labelling each line with who said it. When you want help, you press ⌘↩ from any app: Glint takes a screenshot, gathers everything said since you last asked, adds anything you typed, and sends it to the AI, whose answer streams into the chat panel within a few seconds. When the call ends, Glint writes a title, a summary and tags for the session, and files it in a searchable history, so that a week later you can find the meeting in which a decision was made and read what was actually said.

## What it does

**The overlay.** The control bar and chat panel float above every other window and never take focus away from the app you are working in, which means typing in a document or a shared editor carries on uninterrupted while Glint is open. Glint lives in the menu bar rather than the Dock, and it offers three ways of staying out of the way: invisible mode (on by default) hides every Glint window from screen sharing and recordings, discreet mode fades the overlay until you point at it, and Glance shrinks it to a dot in a corner of the screen. In Glance, when someone else in the call asks a question, a one-line answer appears next to the dot on its own; pointing at it shows the whole answer, and clicking it opens the full panel.

**Asking.** Every ask can include a screenshot, the new part of the transcript and a typed question, and each of these is optional, so Glint works equally well for "what should I say to that?" during a call and "what does this chart show?" with no call at all. A short note can be drawn onto every screenshot sent to the AI, for example "I'm reviewing this pull request, point out bugs first", without it ever appearing on your screen. Modes are saved instructions that steer every answer while active, and alongside the eleven templates you can write your own. Replies can be copied to the clipboard automatically, either in full or just their first code block, ready to paste.

**Transcripts and speakers.** Your microphone and your computer's audio are transcribed separately, so talking over someone does not lose either side, and the call echoing through your speakers is recognised and dropped rather than attributed to you. By default, transcription happens entirely on your Mac, with OpenAI's transcription service available as an alternative. Glint tells voices apart, labelling unknown people "Speaker 1", "Speaker 2" and so on, and after hearing someone for a few lines it asks who they are, with a clip you can play and a Guess button that looks for the name in the conversation itself (someone saying "I'm Dana", or being asked "Dana, what do you think?"). People you name are recognised in later sessions. Recording twenty seconds of your own voice unlocks room mode, which handles meetings in person, where a single microphone hears everyone and Glint has to work out which voice is yours.

**History.** Every session and every chat is saved, grouped by day and searchable by title, summary and tags. A session's page shows its AI-written notes (which you can edit or regenerate), its chat and its full transcript, and it can be resumed later with the timer continuing where it stopped; a standalone chat can likewise be picked up again with Continue chat. If a session sits idle for ten minutes, Glint counts down forty seconds and then ends it, so a forgotten session does not run all afternoon.

**AI providers.** Glint works with four providers: a Claude API key, an OpenAI API key, or an existing Claude or ChatGPT subscription through the `claude` and `codex` command-line tools. If the chosen provider fails before answering, Glint switches to the next one you have ticked, and it makes every failure loud rather than silent: the panel, control bar and menu bar icon turn red, and the reply names what failed and which provider answered instead.

## Privacy and where your data lives

Because there is no Glint server, nothing passes through a third party apart from the AI provider you picked, and that provider receives only what an ask contains: the screenshot, the relevant part of the transcript and your question. Transcription stays on the Mac unless you switch it to OpenAI. Session history, API keys and saved voices are stored on disk and encrypted with the macOS keychain, and deleted sessions go to the Trash rather than disappearing, so a mistaken delete can be undone.

## Using it responsibly

Glint transcribes other people, and in many places recording or transcribing a conversation requires the consent of everyone in it; likewise, the app asks you to save someone's voice only with their agreement. Invisible mode hides Glint from screen sharing, but it does not hide Glint's processes, permissions or audio capture from other software, and it does not make Glint's use permitted where it otherwise would not be: many interviews, exams and assessments forbid outside assistance of any kind, and that rule applies regardless of whether the assistance is visible.

## Current state and limits

Glint runs on macOS 14.2 or later, a requirement set by the system audio capture it relies on, and it is installed with a single Terminal command that builds it from its private GitHub repository; later versions install from inside the app, through Settings. Windows and Linux are not supported yet. Several things a commercial product would have are absent by design or still to come: there are no accounts, billing, shared links or team features, no calendar integration that notices when a meeting is about to start, and no way yet to attach reference files, such as a CV or a job description, to a mode.

Overall, Glint is a private, self-contained meeting assistant that hears the whole conversation, sees your screen when you ask it to, and keeps a searchable record afterwards, built on the premise that this kind of tool can be useful without routing anyone's meetings through someone else's servers.
